**CENTRAL FINDING**: Ghanaian banks have rebuilt earnings and balance sheets after the 2022 debt-exchange shock, but the next phase depends on credit quality and funding costs. In the 20-bank base case, profit rises from GHS 12.92bn in 2026 to GHS 19.29bn in 2030. The 2030 downside is only GHS 7.24bn.

**01 / EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

## The recovery is real, but returns are changing

Findings from a consistent 20-bank historical cohort and separate coverage for three conditional models.

The accepted 20 banks earned GHS 14.03bn in 2025, up from GHS 4.71bn in 2021 and a GHS 5.89bn loss in 2022. Assets reached GHS 403.78bn and equity GHS 56.58bn at FY2025. The Bank of Ghana (BoG) sector total was GHS 446.9bn of assets. This is a broad, but incomplete, sample.

The recovery coincided with recapitalisation, a changing rate environment and the absorption of losses associated with the domestic debt exchange. Balance-sheet growth does not establish the cause of earnings growth. Net lending remained only 30.3% of customer deposits in FY2025 for the matched banks, indicating considerable funding held in other assets.

| GHS bn | 2025 actual | 2026E base | 2030E base | 2030E down | 2030E up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Assets | 403.78 | 481.46 | 818.39 | 694.96 | 929.15 |
| Equity | 56.58 | 63.98 | 113.07 | 93.54 | 123.38 |
| PAT | 14.03 | 12.92 | 19.29 | 7.24 | 26.48 |

The base case shows a softer 2026 profit year, followed by growth from 2027. By 2030, the downside would be about 62% below base earnings and the upside about 37% above base. These are scenario outputs, not probability estimates. Some liquid-asset-heavy banks initially earn more in the downside rate path.

The weakest modeled capital outcomes are concentrated in ADB, CBG, NIB and Societe Generale. GCB is the largest earnings contributor in the 2030 base, so its assumptions deserve disproportionate review. Three excluded banks require a different level of confidence, especially UMB, whose current recapitalised position is not represented by its forecast.

**06 / OUTLOOK INTERPRETATION**

## What each forecast year implies

Iconka interpretation of the signed-off model paths; not a management or regulatory forecast.

### FY2026E: GHS 12.92bn base PAT

Assets GHS 481.46bn; equity GHS 63.98bn. PAT range GHS 10.38bn to 13.59bn across downside and upside. Base closing-equity return 20.2%. A transition year. Lower security yields, repricing and Q4 credit/tax assumptions matter most. Nineteen banks have September actuals; CBG has H1. Preserve the actual interim results when comparing the Q4 estimate.

### FY2027E: GHS 14.60bn base PAT

Assets GHS 549.39bn; equity GHS 74.60bn. PAT range GHS 11.36bn to 16.25bn across downside and upside. Base closing-equity return 19.6%. Recovery in base profit, aided by modeled balance-sheet growth. The downside improves temporarily, but high funding rates and slower GDP constrain a broader recovery.

### FY2028E: GHS 16.01bn base PAT

Assets GHS 628.69bn; equity GHS 86.41bn. PAT range GHS 11.02bn to 18.78bn across downside and upside. Base closing-equity return 18.5%. The spread between scenarios grows. Credit expansion and operating cost control become increasingly important as forecast profits depend less on the observed 2026 anchor.

### FY2029E: GHS 17.21bn base PAT

Assets GHS 717.36bn; equity GHS 98.96bn. PAT range GHS 8.91bn to 22.22bn across downside and upside. Base closing-equity return 17.4%. Downside profit declines sharply while the base continues to grow. Compounding funding, impairment and operating cost stresses dominate the temporary benefit of high rates for some banks.

### FY2030E: GHS 19.29bn base PAT

Assets GHS 818.39bn; equity GHS 113.07bn. PAT range GHS 7.24bn to 26.48bn across downside and upside. Base closing-equity return 17.1%. The base is substantially larger than FY2025 but the wide earnings range highlights uncertainty. Watch concentration and undercapitalised stress cases rather than reading the midpoint as a prediction.

The scenario range is not a confidence interval. No scenario probabilities are assigned; the models do not simulate contagion, a new debt restructuring or an explicit future supervisory response.

## Scaling the model to a sector perspective

The accepted bank set is not the entire banking system. No conditional bank is added to the ordinary projections.

| GHS bn, illustrative | 2026E | 2027E | 2028E | 2029E | 2030E |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20-bank base assets | 481.46 | 549.39 | 628.69 | 717.36 | 818.39 |
| Scale at 90.35% | 532.88 | 608.07 | 695.83 | 793.97 | 905.80 |
| Scale at 87.52% | 550.14 | 627.76 | 718.37 | 819.69 | 935.14 |

If the FY2025 share of industry assets were to remain constant, a purely mechanical extrapolation would place whole-sector base assets at about GHS 905.80bn in 2030. Using the September share gives about GHS 935.14bn. The latter includes only 19 interim reporters; it is a sensitivity, not a superior estimate.

These are Iconka’s illustrative scaling calculations, not outputs approved by the lead. They assume constant coverage, which is unlikely to hold exactly as individual banks recapitalise, merge, expand or contract. The exclusions are not random. No full-sector PAT estimate is justified by the available comparable regulator data; asset share is not a valid proxy for profit share.

The more defensible conclusion is directional: a rising nominal banking balance sheet under each macro path, with much wider dispersion in earnings and capital. From the regulator’s October snapshot, the sector is already above half a trillion cedis in assets. A route toward roughly GHS 0.9tn by 2030 is possible under the model’s base and coverage assumption, but real growth and currency value may differ materially.

### Practical implications

For bank management, monitor loan vintage performance, deposit beta and concentration, reserve-eligible cash and contingent funding. For depositors and businesses, service quality and pricing are separate from model profitability. For policy analysis, lending growth needs to be matched to credit quality rather than targeted in isolation.

## Where the forecast can break

Concentration, credit quality, liquidity, non-interest income and model risk.

| Largest contributions to 2030 base-down PAT gap | GHS m | Share of net gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GCB Bank | 4,231.1 | 35.1% |
| Stanbic Bank Ghana | 1,088.0 | 9.0% |
| Consolidated Bank Ghana | 893.1 | 7.4% |
| Fidelity Bank Ghana | 890.0 | 7.4% |
| Societe Generale Ghana | 773.8 | 6.4% |
| First Atlantic Bank | 636.4 | 5.3% |

GCB alone contributes 26.9% of 2030 base PAT and 59.9% of the net modeled 2025–2030 profit increase. Its forecast is the most consequential individual sensitivity. The sector base-down gap is GHS 12.05bn, with a significant portion coming from GCB and Stanbic.

Credit risk: ADB and NIB report very high FY2025 gross NPL ratios (70.53% and 69.70%). CBG reports 33.42%. A shrinking loan book can raise a ratio even while absolute nonperforming balances fall; very high historic allowances can also make simple linear impairment forecasts misleading.

Funding and liquidity: deposit growth is not equivalent to free cash. The shared 20% reserve requirement interacts with the amount of eligible cash, collateral restrictions, wholesale borrowing and deposit repricing. Several models identify reserve proxy gaps. These are stress screens, not evidence of actual noncompliance.

Forecasting limits: 2026 spreads often use fitted interim results. Trading income fades are judgmental, future FX shocks are incomplete, and balance-sheet and statutory reserve proxies differ by bank. **RESEARCH OPERATIONS / 01**

## How the room was organised

A multi-agent research exercise, not a single-agent financial model.

Claude started the banking goal at 12:29:58 UTC on 25 November. Codex, Gemini and the lead began the first claims within four minutes. DeepSeek joined at 12:48, Helper and Support joined to expand capacity, Luna joined at 13:52 and Papa Kojo Agent at 16:16. The eight research participants worked from a shared GitHub repository, not a common conversational memory.

The lead verified 23 licensed banks against the BoG register, established a common model specification, macro assumptions, ownership and review rules. A bank owner first pushed a claim before starting, reducing duplicate work. Agents stored historical CSVs, sources, assumptions, engines, forecasts and checks in a bank-specific folder. An independent reviewer challenged source coverage, arithmetic, economic assumptions and model behaviour.

When an agent paused because of usage limits, the room used explicitly authorised stewardship rather than silent reassignment. The original author remained credited, the steward repaired or completed the work, and the review evidence remained in the repository. Blocked review substitution required two independent exact-version PASS verdicts and finding-by-finding evidence. Shared reporting used a separate accepted-only gate.

**RESEARCH OPERATIONS / 05**

## Why independent review mattered

Arithmetic passing is necessary, but it does not guarantee sensible forecasts or faithful accounting classifications.

CalBank: early model reviews found overstated interest income and capital, including a right-of-use asset classification problem. Several repair rounds and stewardship changes followed. The final forecast is lower confidence than an audited regulatory return, but the relevant blockers were preserved and resolved.

ADB: a previously accepted model was reopened after Papa Kojo Agent found omitted earnings on cash and placements in the signed FY2025 report, available gross loan/ECL detail, and a capital numerator that did not reflect regulatory capital. Helper corrected it and independent reviewers passed the revised package. This is evidence that acceptance could be revoked when new facts emerged.

Ecobank: six periods of right-of-use asset reclassification were checked. Codex and Support verified exact new fingerprints without having performed the model repair. Forecast values changed even though the model code and assumptions did not; source classification mattered.

OmniBSIC: the final six-note documentation patch did not change numerical output. Despite this, the package fingerprint changed, requiring fresh scoped PASS verdicts by Support and Gemini. UMB and Prudential remained separate because unavailable primary filings could not be recovered after extensive attempts.

**RESEARCH OPERATIONS / 06**

## Evidence boundaries and next research priorities

Iconka’s assessment: what the study does well, and where stronger evidence could change its findings.

The strongest aspects are broad bank coverage, reproducible model packages, historical balance-sheet and income bridges, explicit missing values, a common macro input and a review process that caught substantive mistakes. The accepted-only roll-up also avoids mixing incomparable data or quietly admitting incomplete banks.

The greatest weaknesses are reliance on simplified forecast drivers, estimated capital and liquidity proxies, incomplete regulatory notes, and a short observable calibration window. Source spot-checks cannot replace an audit. Some bank README headings still say needs-review even though the signed-off claim and manifest later passed; the final status is governed by the exact accepted package and lead decision, not an old heading.

### Priority follow-up work

1. Retrieve Prudential September 2026, OmniBSIC FY2021 and UMB FY2025/2026 issuer statements; re-review and rebind those packages before admission to an ordinary roll-up. For UMB specifically, reconcile the post-recapitalisation balance sheet and regulatory capital recognition.

2. Refresh FY2026 actuals once published. Compare actual Q4 income and balance sheets with the forecast. Report signed forecast errors bank by bank, identify drivers and revise assumptions only where the evidence supports it.

3. Add a monthly deposit and asset repricing schedule, explicit sovereign holdings and bond maturities, loan vintage/default/migration dynamics, deposit concentration and liquidity runoff. Use regulatory RWA and eligible cash disclosures where possible.

4. Build an evidence-backed macro history, including missing CPI and bill yields, then backtest the forecasting framework before applying it to longer horizons. Use explicit alternate FX paths and funding stress. Do not assign probabilities without a defensible calibration.

5. Track model and source provenance separately from operational coordination. Record missing links, direct visual checks and each model’s source vintage. Retain a versioned, immutable acceptance manifest for later reruns.

**CONCLUSION**: The models provide a useful map of Ghanaian banking, rather than an exact picture of future performance. The most credible expectation is a larger system in nominal cedis with diverging bank profitability and a material downside for weaker portfolios. Use the forecasts to ask sharper questions, update them with actual results, and preserve their uncertainty.
