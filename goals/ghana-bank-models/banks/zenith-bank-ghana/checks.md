## 2. Calibration disclosure (a fit, not a validation)

- FY2026E interest income is **anchored** on 9M-2026 x 12/9 = 2,225.8m. The forward formula is: 364-day bill + 6.0 pts on average net loans, plus on opening liquid assets a fast bucket (58.35% of securities repricing within 3 months in the FY2025 schedule) at the 91-day bill + 0.5 pts and a slow bucket at 11.6%. It gives 2,224.9m for FY2026E, a difference of -0.9m (-0.0%). The slow yield was solved to land on the anchor, so this match is by construction; the fast share is observed (FY2025 audited repricing schedule, as read by an independent reviewer).
- FY2026E interest expense is anchored on 9M-2026 x 12/9 = 738.4m. The cost-of-funds ratio 0.204 x an assumed 14.3% 2026 average policy rate x average funding 25,273.6m gives 737.3m (-0.1%). The 14.3% average is taken from SCB's assumptions basis and was not re-verified.
- The macro file's FY2026E bill rates (91-day 5.81%, 364-day 11.01%) are lower than rates earlier in 2026, so yields from FY2027E depend on the fitted spreads staying stable. That is untested.

## 3b. Scenario ordering and a tax sensitivity

| Year | PAT downside | PAT base | PAT upside | Ordered downside < base < upside? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FY2026E | 1,094 | 1,094 | 1,094 | yes |
| FY2027E | 1,199 | 1,133 | 1,140 | NO (inverted) |
| FY2028E | 1,405 | 1,407 | 1,436 | yes |
| FY2029E | 1,417 | 1,680 | 1,811 | yes |
| FY2030E | 1,554 | 2,016 | 2,222 | yes |

Profit is not ordered downside < base < upside in FY2027E. This is a property of the shared macro file, not an error in the arithmetic: the downside has higher policy and bill rates (policy rate 14% in FY2027E-FY2028E vs 10% in base and 9% in upside), so the upside earns slightly less than the base on rate-sensitive assets, and Zenith holds about 9,000m of liquid assets that reprice with bills while the cost-of-funds ratio is 0.204 of the policy rate, so higher rates lift net interest income more than funding cost. The downside still carries a cost of risk of 4% and zero dividends, so equity is highest in the downside by FY2030E. Reviewers should challenge whether a rate-rise scenario deserves this asset sensitivity (it is the same formula as in banks/standard-chartered-ghana).

Tax sensitivity (unverified alternative): if the FY2026E tax rate were 28% (9M-2026 17.8% plus the 10.3-point step seen between 9M-2025 and FY2025), FY2026E profit after tax would be 1,194m instead of 1,094m.
